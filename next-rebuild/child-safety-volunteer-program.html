Our guiding rule

Protect children without putting volunteers, image contributors, or the public at risk. TikNation volunteers are educators and responsible reporters—not undercover investigators, law enforcement, or vigilantes.

Important boundary: TikNation will not authorize volunteers to impersonate minors, bait or privately engage suspected offenders, arrange meetings, solicit confessions, run stings on other platforms, threaten or expose people, or livestream confrontations. Any genuine undercover investigation must be conducted by appropriately authorized professionals under their own legal and safeguarding procedures.

Real-world support

Responsible Reporting

Volunteers may report concerning public content they encounter, preserve non-explicit account details and URLs, and refer concerns to platform safety teams or appropriate authorities. They must not initiate contact to test or entrap anyone.

What real-world volunteers may do

  • Recognize grooming, coercion, sextortion, trafficking indicators, and unsafe contact patterns through approved training.
  • Report concerning material through the platform where it appears and appropriate official reporting channels.
  • Record only necessary, non-explicit facts such as public account names, URLs, dates, times, and a concise description of what they directly observed.
  • Help families and young people find trusted support, without promising secrecy or trying to investigate.
  • Refer credible concerns to qualified child-protection organizations or authorized authorities.
Do not: create a false child profile; contact or bait a suspected offender; move a conversation to private channels to “collect proof”; ask a child to send images; download, screenshot, upload, forward, or circulate sexual material involving minors; arrange a meeting; publish accusations or personal information; or mobilize viewers against someone.

Controlled simulations inside TikNation

Simulations should teach recognition and safe decision-making—not rehearse undercover tactics. Every exercise is fictional, visibly identified as training, and kept separate from real accounts and real conversations.

  1. Fictional scenario. Use invented adult characters, invented usernames, and scripted non-explicit messages. Never base a scenario on an identifiable child or real suspect.
  2. Practice safe decisions. Participants identify warning signs, end unsafe interactions, preserve appropriate non-explicit context, and choose the correct reporting route.
  3. Supervisor review. A trained moderator reviews the exercise and provides feedback. No real-world contact is part of the exercise.
  4. Clear completion status. A completed simulation is a platform learning record, not investigative certification or authority to conduct an operation. Voice effects are for clearly identified adult-only exercises; no child impersonation or real-suspect contact is permitted.

Adult image contributor protections

A person's photograph or video is part of their identity. Permission to use an image is not permission to impersonate that person, speak on their behalf, or contact anyone using their likeness.

  • Adults only: contributors must be 18 or older and complete an appropriate private age/identity check before any future participation.
  • Separate, specific consent: permission must identify each asset, exact educational purpose, where it may appear, who may access it, and how long permission lasts.
  • Preview before approval: the contributor sees the exact proposed presentation and approves it before use.
  • No suspect-facing impersonation: contributed likenesses may only be considered for clearly disclosed educational content or fictional adult simulations inside TikNation—not for deceptive real-world conversations with suspected offenders.
  • No sexualized manipulation: no intimate, sexualized, humiliating, or deceptive AI alteration; no childlike styling; no use in any context involving sexual content about minors.
  • Access accountability: if this feature is built, every authorized access and use must be logged, limited to named personnel, and reviewable by the contributor.
  • Withdrawal: contributors may revoke future permission. TikNation must stop new use promptly and explain any limited copies that cannot technically be recalled, such as material already lawfully published or cached by third parties.
  • No pressure or payment condition: participation must be voluntary, with a clear way to decline or withdraw without penalty.
Not active yet: TikNation is not currently accepting photographs, videos, identity documents, or image-use permissions for this program. Do not send these materials through messages, applications, or support tickets. A secure consent and deletion workflow must be built and independently tested before any collection begins.

Qualification and oversight

  1. Applicant is at least 18 and uses their own verified TikNation account.
  2. Completes the Child Safety Academy and passes its 18-question assessment with at least 15 correct answers.
  3. Accepts the Child Safety Advocate Code of Conduct.
  4. Completes a separate program-specific training and safeguarding review before any simulation role is considered.
  5. Receives explicit Founder approval for the exact role and permitted activities.
  6. Uses only approved training environments and follows audit, privacy, complaint, and suspension procedures.

Advocate approval does not automatically qualify someone as a simulation participant or image contributor. No role grants access to children's private messages, private account data, identity records, or moderation controls.

When a real concern needs reporting

If a child is in immediate danger in the United States, call 911. For suspected online child sexual exploitation, use the NCMEC CyberTipline ↗. For suspected human trafficking in the United States, contact the National Human Trafficking Hotline at 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733. Follow the relevant agency's instructions; do not conduct your own investigation.

For other countries, use the relevant local emergency, child-protection, or law-enforcement reporting service.

What happens next

This page establishes the program's safety boundaries and future design. It does not enroll volunteers, authorize decoy activity, collect contributor images, or create an investigative team. Before any additional role opens, TikNation needs a separate application, role-specific training, documented consent controls, privacy and legal review, secure audit records, complaint handling, and an end-to-end safety test.
