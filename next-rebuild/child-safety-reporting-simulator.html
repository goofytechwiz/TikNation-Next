INTERACTIVE FICTIONAL PRACTICE

Child Safety Reporting Simulator

Practice choosing safe, proportionate responses to fictional situations. The goal is not to investigate or decide guilt—it is to protect people, preserve privacy, and route concerns to the right help.

Before you begin

Immediate danger comes first. In the United States, call 911 if a child or anyone else may be in immediate danger. Do not wait for TikNation or a volunteer response.
  • All scenarios are fictional and contain no real targets or contact instructions.
  • Do not message, bait, impersonate, follow, confront, expose, or investigate anyone.
  • Never request, download, screenshot, save, upload, or forward sexual material involving a minor.
  • Use only safe, non-explicit facts and approved reporting channels.

This is a browser-based learning exercise. Answers and scores are not sent to or saved in your TikNation account. It is not an investigation credential or emergency service.

Scenario 1 · A child may be in immediate danger

URGENT ESCALATION

A member writes that their younger sibling is with an adult who has threatened them and may be harmed tonight. The member shares a general location but asks you to keep it secret.

What is the safest response?

Why: Immediate danger requires emergency services now. Do not dispatch volunteers or attempt a rescue. Share only necessary information through approved channels.

Scenario 2 · A suspicious online approach

GROOMING CONCERN

A parent reports that an adult account repeatedly asks their teen to keep conversations secret and move to another app. No explicit image is attached.

What should you do?

Why: Secrecy and attempts to move a young person off-platform can be warning signs, not proof by themselves. Report facts; do not bait or publicly accuse.

Scenario 3 · Sexual material involving a minor

DO NOT HANDLE MATERIAL

A user says they have an explicit image of someone who may be under 18 and offers to send it to you so you can decide whether it is real.

What is the safest response?

Why: Never request, download, save, screenshot, upload, or forward sexual material involving minors. Do not ask someone else to handle it for you. Follow official reporting instructions.

Scenario 4 · A possible missing child

MISSING-CHILD REPORT

A guardian says their child has not returned home and cannot be reached. They ask TikNation volunteers to search local parks before contacting police.

What is the safest guidance?

Why: There is no 24-hour waiting requirement to report a missing child in the U.S. Volunteers should not organize searches or publish identifying information.

Scenario 5 · One possible trafficking indicator

AVOID ASSUMPTIONS

You see an adult answering questions for another adult and controlling their phone. You do not know their relationship or circumstances. Someone in chat says, “That's definitely trafficking—go confront them.”

What is the best response?

Why: Indicators are not proof. Avoid profiling based on appearance, nationality, poverty, or assumptions. Do not approach, follow, or attempt to remove anyone.

Scenario 6 · A complaint about a volunteer

ACCOUNTABILITY

A member reports that an approved volunteer asked a teen to move to a private messaging app. The volunteer is your friend and asks you to delete the complaint.

What should you do?

Why: Reports about staff or volunteers must be handled impartially. A conflict of interest should be disclosed; the person involved should not control the review.

Complete the practice

Choose one answer in each of the six scenarios, then check your results. You can reveal the learning point for each scenario after checking.
