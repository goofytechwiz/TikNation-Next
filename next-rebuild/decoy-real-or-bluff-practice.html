Real or Bluff? Safety Scenario Practice

Practice distinguishing intimidation, possible genuine risks, and unsupported claims. These scenarios are fictional. Do not contact or investigate anyone.

Scenario 1

A stranger says they know your address and demands money, but provides no proof.

Recommended response

Treat the demand as intimidation; do not pay or engage. The address claim may be true or false. Preserve the message, report it, review privacy, and seek help if there is a specific threat.

Threat is coercive; personal-data claim needs independent assessment.

Scenario 2

A profile appears suspicious and asks you to move to an encrypted chat.

Recommended response

Do not bait or test the profile. Do not continue contact to gather proof. Use platform reporting and tell the designated supervisor if it arose in an approved context.

Suspicion is not proof. Volunteers do not conduct undercover contact.

Scenario 3

A person says they are a police officer and asks you to send them private account data through direct message.

Recommended response

Do not disclose private data. Verify any official request through established organizational/legal channels and escalate to the privacy lead.

A claim of authority in a message is not verification.

Scenario 4

Someone says they have an intimate image and threatens to publish it unless paid.

Recommended response

Do not pay, send more, or comply. Preserve safe non-explicit evidence, report the account, secure accounts, and seek trusted support. If a minor is involved, use appropriate child-protection reporting channels.

The threat may be real or exaggerated; never assume either.

Remember

These are fictional learning exercises only. Never apply them by contacting a real person, impersonating a minor, arranging a meeting, confronting someone, or collecting private information.
