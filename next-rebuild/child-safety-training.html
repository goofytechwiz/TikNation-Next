Safety comes before investigation

Advocates help by recognizing concerning behavior, reporting it through safe channels, and supporting young people. They do not run stings, impersonate children, solicit confessions, confront suspects, or investigate independently.

Never create or request sexual content involving a minor. Do not download, screenshot, forward, upload, or circulate child sexual abuse material. Do not ask a child to send it to you. If such material appears, stop interacting with it and report it through the appropriate official channel.

1 · Recognize patterns, not stereotypes

A single behavior does not prove abuse. Look at context and repeated boundary violations.

Boundary testing

Repeatedly asking a young person to keep secrets, hide conversations, or ignore a caregiver's safety rules.

Isolation

Pressuring them to move to private or disappearing channels, or discouraging them from talking to trusted adults.

Sexualized pressure

Requests for intimate images, sexual conversations, or attempts to normalize sexual behavior with a minor.

Coercion

Threats, blackmail, gifts used as leverage, or pressure to comply with demands.

These are warning signs to report—not a checklist for deciding guilt. Do not confront or publicly accuse someone based on suspicion.

2 · What to do when a concern appears

  1. Prioritize immediate safety. If someone is in immediate danger in the United States, call 911. Do not delay for a platform report.
  2. Use TikNation's report flow. Submit the account name, relevant platform link, approximate time, and a concise factual description.
  3. Preserve safely. Keep the original platform URL, account handle, timestamps, and report reference. Do not copy or redistribute illegal material.
  4. Stop direct engagement. Do not bait, threaten, warn, expose, or arrange a meeting with the suspected person.
  5. Escalate appropriately. In the United States, suspected online child exploitation can be reported to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) CyberTipline.
Official U.S. reporting: NCMEC CyberTipline ↗. For immediate danger, call 911. For situations outside the United States, use the appropriate local child-protection or law-enforcement reporting channel.

3 · Protect privacy and evidence integrity

  • Record only what is necessary to explain the concern.
  • Keep reports factual: separate what you directly observed from what you infer.
  • Do not share a report in public chats, creator groups, social media, or with unrelated staff.
  • Never request identity documents, home addresses, passwords, or private case files from a young person.
  • Do not upload child sexual abuse material to TikNation or to this training page.
  • Use official reporting channels and follow their instructions about evidence preservation.
TikNation's advocate designation is not a law-enforcement credential and does not grant access to private messages, hidden user data, or moderation powers.
Core lesson

5 · Understand grooming and online exploitation

Online grooming is a process in which someone builds or misuses trust to manipulate a young person for exploitation. It may happen gradually or very quickly. It can begin in social media, livestream chats, direct messages, or online games. The person may be a stranger—or someone the young person already knows.

False identity and trust

Someone may lie about their age, identity, intentions, or relationship to the young person. A friendly profile or shared interests do not prove someone is safe.

Secrecy and isolation

They may encourage secret conversations, move chats to private apps, or tell the young person that trusted adults “wouldn't understand.”

Pressure and boundary crossing

They may introduce sexual topics, request intimate images, or push a young person to do things that feel uncomfortable.

Threats and sextortion

They may threaten to share an image, expose private information, or harm someone unless the young person sends money, more images, or obeys demands.

Important: A young person is never to blame for being manipulated, threatened, or exploited. Sending money or complying with demands does not guarantee the threats will stop. Encourage them to stop responding, preserve non-explicit account details and URLs where safe, get help from a trusted adult, and report. Never ask them to show or forward intimate material.

6 · What child trafficking means

Human trafficking involves exploiting a person through force, fraud, or coercion. When a person under 18 is induced to perform a commercial sex act, federal U.S. law treats it as child sex trafficking even if force, fraud, or coercion is not proven. Child trafficking can also involve forced labor or other forms of exploitation.

Trafficking is not always a stranger grabbing a child off the street. A trafficker may be someone the child knows, someone who pretends to be a romantic partner, or someone who uses promises of money, gifts, housing, work, travel, attention, or protection. Exploitation can occur online and offline, and a young person may not recognize what is happening or may feel unable to leave.

Warning signs that deserve a careful, supportive response

  • Someone else controls a young person's money, phone, identification, movements, or communications.
  • A young person seems afraid of a person who accompanies them or is not free to speak privately.
  • There are threats, debts, blackmail, or pressure connected to work, travel, housing, gifts, or sexual activity.
  • A person is being moved, recruited, or pressured into exploitative situations.

No single sign proves trafficking. Avoid assumptions based on clothing, neighborhood, identity, immigration status, or whether someone appears “cooperative.” Listen, prioritize safety, and refer concerns to trained authorities.

Do not investigate, interview repeatedly, promise rescue, or confront a suspected trafficker. Do not attempt to remove someone yourself. If there is immediate danger, contact emergency services.

7 · Protect yourself online: a practical safety plan

Keep personal details private

Do not share your home address, school, daily routine, live location, phone number, passwords, travel plans, or identifying details with people you do not know and trust offline.

Use strong account security

Use unique passwords, turn on multi-factor authentication, keep apps updated, and never share login codes—even with someone claiming to be platform support.

Check privacy settings

Limit who can message, tag, mention, view stories, join live sessions, or see your location. Review followers and connected apps regularly.

Trust your discomfort

You can say no, end a chat, block, and report. You do not owe anyone photos, secrets, money, a meeting, or an explanation.

  • Do not meet an online contact alone. Talk with a trusted adult and follow family safety rules.
  • Be cautious of offers that require secrecy, urgent decisions, travel, money transfers, or private images.
  • If someone threatens you, do not negotiate alone or send more material. Get help quickly.
  • If a friend tells you something worrying, listen calmly, believe them, avoid blame, and help them reach a safe adult.

Remember: Privacy tools help, but responsibility does not belong to children alone. Platforms, adults, and communities must build safer spaces and respond when concerns are raised.

9 · If something happens: what to say and do

Helpful words: “I'm glad you told me. This isn't your fault. You are not in trouble. Let's find a safe adult who can help.”

  • Stay calm and listen. Let the young person share in their own words; do not interrogate them or ask leading questions.
  • Ask only what is needed to understand immediate safety: “Are you in danger right now?” and “Is the person trying to meet you?”
  • Do not promise secrecy. Explain that you may need to involve people whose job is to protect them.
  • Do not view, download, screenshot, forward, or upload explicit images of minors. Do not ask the young person to send them to prove what happened.
  • Report through the platform and appropriate authorities. In the U.S., use the NCMEC CyberTipline for suspected online child exploitation.
Emergency: If someone is in immediate danger in the U.S., call 911. NCMEC CyberTipline: report.cybertip.org ↗. For trafficking concerns, the U.S. National Human Trafficking Hotline is 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733; call 911 for immediate danger.
New lesson

10 · AI, deepfakes and online impersonation

AI tools can make convincing fake profile photos, voices, messages, and videos. A familiar face or voice is not proof that a message is genuine. Images and recordings can be manipulated, taken out of context, or used to pressure someone.

Pause before trusting

Verify unexpected requests through a separate, known contact method. Do not use the phone number, link, or account supplied in the suspicious message.

Watch for urgent pressure

Requests for secrecy, money, gift cards, login codes, private images, or immediate action deserve caution—even when the voice or profile looks familiar.

Protect your likeness

Limit public personal photos and identifying details. Review privacy settings and ask trusted adults for help if someone misuses your image or voice.

Respond safely

Do not repost humiliating or intimate fake content. Save non-explicit URLs, account names, and timestamps where safe, report it, and seek trusted support.

For adults and advocates: Never create, request, or circulate sexualized synthetic content depicting a minor. Do not ask a young person to send intimate material to prove an allegation. Report the account and content through appropriate official channels.
New lesson

11 · Parent and guardian learning center

Children are more likely to ask for help when they expect calm support rather than punishment. Make online safety an ongoing conversation, not a one-time warning.

Start with connection

  • Ask open, non-judgmental questions about the apps, games, creators, and people your child interacts with.
  • Agree on family rules for messaging, livestreams, location sharing, downloads, and in-person meetups.
  • Tell them they can come to you after a mistake or uncomfortable interaction without being blamed.

Notice changes without jumping to conclusions

Sudden secrecy, fear, distress, unexplained gifts, or pressure from an online contact may justify a gentle conversation. None of these alone proves exploitation. Avoid accusations, threats, or taking away the child's only route to support.

If a child tells you something happened

  • Stay calm. Say: “Thank you for telling me. You are not in trouble. This is not your fault.”
  • Ask whether they are in immediate danger. Do not repeatedly question them or conduct your own investigation.
  • Do not view, save, forward, or request explicit images. Preserve non-explicit account details and URLs where safe.
  • Use the platform's reporting tools and contact the appropriate child-protection authority. Call emergency services for immediate danger.

13 · Safety Academy learning pathway

Use these suggested stages to build knowledge gradually. The stages are educational milestones, not professional qualifications or permission to investigate.

Level 1 · Foundations

Privacy, boundaries, safe messaging, blocking and reporting, and how to ask a trusted adult for help.

Suggested audience: young people and families, with age-appropriate supervision.

Level 2 · Recognize risks

Grooming, sextortion, trafficking awareness, impersonation, deepfakes, scams, and coercive behavior.

Suggested audience: parents, guardians, educators, moderators, and adult advocates.

Level 3 · Respond responsibly

Supportive conversations, factual platform reports, privacy protection, referral pathways, and avoiding vigilantism.

Suggested audience: approved adult advocates and platform safety teams.

Young people · age-appropriate learning

Teen Safety Hub

You deserve to feel safe online. You can stop a conversation, leave a livestream, block an account, and ask for help—even if you started the conversation, accepted a gift, shared information, or made a mistake. You are not to blame if someone manipulates or threatens you.

When someone asks for secrecy

A person who insists you hide a friendship, move to private chats, or keep messages from trusted adults may be testing your boundaries. Talk to a safe adult.

When someone asks for images

You never owe anyone an intimate image. Do not send more if pressured. Do not forward or show intimate images of minors to friends or helpers. Tell a trusted adult and use safe reporting channels.

When someone offers gifts or money

A gift does not create a debt. Be cautious if someone expects secrecy, private contact, images, a meeting, or obedience in return.

When you need help

Choose an adult who listens and helps you feel safe—a parent, guardian, school counselor, teacher, or another trusted adult. If the first person does not listen, tell another safe adult.

Quick plan: Stop replying if safe, block/report the account, tell a trusted adult, and seek emergency help if you are in immediate danger. You do not have to handle it alone.

Urgent support guide

Sextortion: if someone is threatening you

You are not to blame. Threats and blackmail are the other person's responsibility. You do not have to pay, send more images, or keep the threat secret.
  1. Get support now. Tell a trusted adult or safe person. If there is immediate physical danger, call emergency services.
  2. Do not comply with demands. Paying or sending more material does not guarantee the threats will stop.
  3. Stop engaging when safe. Do not threaten, negotiate, retaliate, or arrange to meet the person.
  4. Keep non-explicit details. Where safe, note usernames, profile links, URLs, dates, times, and threatening messages. Do not download, screenshot, forward, upload, or share sexual images of anyone under 18.
  5. Report and get specialist help. Use the platform's report tools and appropriate official channels. In the U.S., NCMEC's CyberTipline accepts reports of suspected online child exploitation.

For help removing or limiting the spread of explicit images taken when someone was under 18, NCMEC provides Take It Down. Use the official service's instructions; do not upload or send the image to TikNation or another person. The service uses a hash generated on the user's device.

For immediate danger in the United States, call 911. For suspected trafficking, the National Human Trafficking Hotline is 1-888-373-7888 or text 233733. These are external services, not TikNation support lines.

Practice · fictional examples

Interactive scenario practice

These fictional situations help learners practice safe decisions. They do not teach members to investigate, confront, entrap, or identify suspects.

Scenario 1 · A private-message request

A livestream guest repeatedly asks a teen to leave the public chat and keep the conversation secret. The safest response is to stop the interaction, use platform reporting, and tell a trusted adult or authorized moderator—not to follow the guest into private messages.

Scenario 2 · A threat to expose images

Someone demands money and threatens to publish private images. The young person deserves support without blame. They should not pay or send more material, should stop engaging when safe, and should seek trusted help and official reporting.

Scenario 3 · A suspicious report

A moderator receives an allegation they cannot verify. They should protect the reporter's privacy, record only relevant non-explicit facts, and escalate through authorized review. They must not publicly accuse the account or recruit users to investigate.

Inclusive learning

Accessible learning & language support

  • Use clear headings, short paragraphs, descriptive link labels, and keyboard-operable expandable lessons.
  • When videos are recommended, choose versions with accurate captions and provide a transcript or equivalent written explanation where the publisher offers one.
  • Do not rely on color, sound, or animation alone to communicate an important safety instruction.
  • Allow learners to pause, take breaks, and skip upsetting material without losing access to help information.
  • Use browser translation only as a convenience; critical safety and emergency instructions should be checked by a qualified human translator before official translated versions are published.

This section is an accessibility standard for the Academy, not a claim that every external video has captions or that the entire platform has been independently audited.

Youth protection standard

Youth Protection & Privacy Standard

TikNation should collect the minimum information needed to receive and review a safety report, restrict access to authorized staff, and explain how information is handled in plain language.

  • Minimize: Do not ask young people to provide passwords, identity documents, home addresses, explicit material, or unrelated private details.
  • Limit access: Report details should be visible only to staff with a legitimate safety role and appropriate permissions.
  • Protect reporters: Do not reveal a reporter's identity to unrelated users or permit retaliation.
  • Be transparent: The platform's privacy notice should explain what is collected, why, who can access it, retention practices, and how to request help.
  • Handle fairly: Reports are allegations, not findings. Review them consistently and do not automatically punish an account solely because a report was filed.
  • Escalate responsibly: Use official channels for emergencies and suspected exploitation; do not conduct vigilante investigations.
Implementation note: This is the Academy's intended standard. The presence of this page does not prove every platform workflow, access permission, retention setting, or privacy notice already meets it. Those controls must be separately audited and tested before TikNation claims compliance.
Content governance

Annual resource review

Safety resources and external reporting routes can change. TikNation should assign a named owner to review this Academy at least annually and whenever a major law, official service, or platform policy changes.

  • Check every external link and confirm it still belongs to the named organization.
  • Review emergency contacts and reporting instructions for each country or region the Academy serves.
  • Check that videos remain age-appropriate, accessible, and available with captions or transcripts where offered.
  • Review quiz answers, terminology, and guidance with qualified child-safety and privacy specialists.
  • Record the review date, reviewer role, sources checked, changes made, and next review date in a maintained editorial log.

No review date is claimed here. A review should only be marked complete after a person has actually checked the materials.

Staff learning

14 · Moderator response training

Moderators should respond consistently, protect privacy, and escalate concerns through approved internal routes. They are not investigators and should not attempt to determine guilt.

Receive a report calmly

Thank the reporter, avoid blame, do not promise absolute secrecy, and check whether anyone appears to be in immediate danger. Do not ask for explicit material or unnecessary personal details.

Record only relevant facts

Capture the reported account identifier, content URL or platform reference, approximate time, report category, and a concise description of what was observed. Distinguish direct observations from assumptions. Keep access limited to authorized staff.

Escalate—do not investigate

Use TikNation's approved moderation escalation process. Do not create decoy accounts, impersonate minors, message a suspected offender, arrange meetings, conduct public callouts, or ask community members to investigate.

Handle illegal material safely

Never download, screenshot, forward, upload, or circulate child sexual abuse material. Never ask a reporter or child to send it. Stop viewing/interacting and follow official reporting instructions. Do not put explicit material into internal notes or chat.

Use fair, proportionate moderation

A report is an allegation, not proof. Follow platform policy, preserve due process, document the moderation rationale, and do not impose automatic penalties solely because a report was submitted. Escalate urgent risks promptly.

Protect the reporter and affected young person

Do not reveal who reported, share case details with unrelated staff, or make promises about outcomes. Offer the platform's reporting and support routes, and refer emergencies to emergency services.

This training does not authorize moderators to access private information beyond their assigned platform permissions or to act as law enforcement, social workers, or forensic examiners.
Quality & safety check

16 · Safety readiness review

Before a training release is considered ready, the platform owner or designated reviewer should verify each item in the live experience. These are review prompts, not automatic technical checks.

  • Every topic link opens the correct lesson on desktop and a phone.
  • Expandable lessons open and close using touch, keyboard, and screen readers.
  • Quiz answers provide useful explanations and the score reflects all questions.
  • External resources load, are from trusted organizations, and are reviewed for age suitability.
  • Emergency and reporting routes are visible and clearly distinguish immediate danger from non-emergency reports.
  • Advocate and Founder-only tools enforce access restrictions on the server, not just in page scripts.
  • Moderators know the escalation route and are instructed not to investigate or circulate illegal material.
  • Privacy notices explain what report information is collected, who can access it, and how long it is retained.
  • Any progress tracking or completion badge is withheld until account-based storage, access controls, and cross-device behavior are implemented and tested.
A checklist on this page does not itself verify deployment, access controls, external links, or account permissions. Those require separate testing.
