Start with the safest response

Pause before responding. Do not send money, verification codes, passwords, identity documents, or private images to someone who contacts you unexpectedly. Use the platform's report and block tools, secure your account, and get help if there is a credible threat.

If someone is in immediate physical danger, call 911 in the United States. TikNation reports and moderator tools are not emergency services.
IDENTITY & IMPERSONATION

1 · Fake profiles and stolen identities

  • Check the account handle and profile history; a familiar display name or photo does not prove identity.
  • Contact the real person through a separate, previously trusted channel—not through links or numbers supplied by the suspicious account.
  • Report the impersonating profile and include its username and profile URL.
  • Ask friends not to engage, pay, or share the account's messages.

Do not publicly accuse someone based only on resemblance or an unverified report. Let the review process assess the evidence.

AI & SYNTHETIC MEDIA

2 · Cloned voices and deepfakes

  • A familiar voice, face, or video can be copied or manipulated.
  • Verify urgent requests for money or secrecy through a second trusted channel.
  • Do not rely on a single visual clue or an AI detector as proof.
  • Preserve non-explicit context such as account names, URLs, dates, and message text; report the content.

Never create, request, or share sexualized synthetic imagery of a minor. Report suspected child exploitation through the official reporting routes in the Safety Center.

DOXXING & LOCATION

3 · Protect personal information

  • Review public posts for addresses, school or workplace details, travel plans, vehicle plates, and recognizable location clues.
  • Disable unnecessary location sharing and avoid posting real-time whereabouts.
  • Ask trusted contacts not to tag your live location or reveal private details.
  • If private information is posted, capture the URL and time without resharing it, report it, and review account security.
HARASSMENT & COORDINATED ABUSE

4 · Raids, dogpiles, and repeat contact

  • Use available chat slow mode, follower or message restrictions, mute, block, and moderator escalation tools.
  • Do not retaliate, dox, threaten, or organize counter-harassment.
  • Keep a simple timeline of accounts, URLs, dates, and actions taken.
  • Report patterns, not just isolated messages; avoid assuming several accounts belong to one person without evidence.
LIVE EMERGENCY RESPONSE

5 · When a LIVE session becomes unsafe

  1. Tell a moderator and use available mute, remove-guest, block, or report controls.
  2. If a serious threat or personal information appears, stop the stream using the creator's available controls.
  3. Do not invite viewers to confront or identify the person.
  4. After the stream, secure the account, submit a report, and preserve non-explicit details.

Actual control availability depends on the current LIVE implementation and assigned role. This lesson does not certify that every emergency control is deployed.

ACCOUNT TAKEOVER

6 · If a creator account is compromised

  • Use the official account recovery route from a trusted device.
  • Change the password on the account and any reused accounts; enable two-step verification.
  • Review active sessions and revoke unfamiliar ones where supported.
  • Warn followers through a verified channel if scam messages were sent.
  • Never give support staff a password, one-time code, or recovery key.

What TikNation should enforce—not merely describe

These are engineering and moderation requirements, not a claim that every control is already live:

  • Clear impersonation reporting with a safe path for the person whose identity is copied.
  • Role-based LIVE controls, reliable stream-stop behavior, and an auditable record of moderator actions.
  • Privacy defaults that reduce exposure of precise location and sensitive profile details, especially for younger members.
  • Rate limits and abuse-pattern review that minimize false positives and do not automatically punish someone based only on reports.
  • Secure account recovery, strong authentication for privileged roles, and clear session-revocation behavior.
  • Appeals and human review for consequential enforcement decisions.
We should collect only the information needed to investigate a report, restrict access to it, set a documented retention period, and log privileged access. Safety must not become indiscriminate surveillance.
