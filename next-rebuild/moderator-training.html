14 · Moderator response training

Moderators should respond consistently, protect privacy, and escalate concerns through approved internal routes. They are not investigators and should not attempt to determine guilt.

Receive a report calmly

Thank the reporter, avoid blame, do not promise absolute secrecy, and check whether anyone appears to be in immediate danger. Do not ask for explicit material or unnecessary personal details.

Record only relevant facts

Capture the reported account identifier, content URL or platform reference, approximate time, report category, and a concise description of what was observed. Distinguish direct observations from assumptions. Keep access limited to authorized staff.

Escalate—do not investigate

Use TikNation's approved moderation escalation process. Do not create decoy accounts, impersonate minors, message a suspected offender, arrange meetings, conduct public callouts, or ask community members to investigate.

Handle illegal material safely

Never download, screenshot, forward, upload, or circulate child sexual abuse material. Never ask a reporter or child to send it. Stop viewing/interacting and follow official reporting instructions. Do not put explicit material into internal notes or chat.

Use fair, proportionate moderation

A report is an allegation, not proof. Follow platform policy, preserve due process, document the moderation rationale, and do not impose automatic penalties solely because a report was submitted. Escalate urgent risks promptly.

Protect the reporter and affected young person

Do not reveal who reported, share case details with unrelated staff, or make promises about outcomes. Offer the platform's reporting and support routes, and refer emergencies to emergency services.

This training does not authorize moderators to access private information beyond their assigned platform permissions or to act as law enforcement, social workers, or forensic examiners.
